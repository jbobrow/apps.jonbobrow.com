Cookbo

Every recipe you cook, in one place.

Share a recipe link and Cookbo keeps the recipe and leaves the rest: the title, photo, ingredients and steps. No ads, no accounts, and your recipes stay yours as plain files.

How it came together Draft

A Sunday brunch request

Cookbo started as a request from my partner over Sunday brunch: I just want to have all of my recipes in one place.

Just the recipe

My own gripe with online recipes is everything around them: the backstory, the ads, the pop-ups. I just want the recipe. So the first big feature was the import share extension. Tap Share on a recipe page, tap Cookbo, and only the ingredients, steps, times and a photo come through.

Your recipes, your files

I’m a fan of Obsidian and its flat-file approach: your data is your data. Cookbo keeps every recipe as a human-readable Markdown file in a folder you can open yourself, and iCloud syncs it across your Apple devices, so there’s no account to make. It runs on iPhone, iPad and Mac, so typing a recipe in can happen at a keyboard and cooking from it can happen on a phone.

In the kitchen I also want to check off what I already have and the steps I’ve already done. Those checkmarks are saved in the recipe file too.

Adding and taking away

Cookbo has grown in bursts, and not everything stayed. Sharing a recipe by tapping two phones together lasted one day; the commit that removed it says it was too fancy and not needed.

Smart collections

As recipes piled up, I added smart collections: Recently Added, This Week for meal planners, and Favorites, with the recipe you looked at last pinned to the top.

Cook mode

Last came cook mode, which has my favorite details in the app. First, an overview: a visual timeline of the whole cook, so you know what you’re getting into. Next, prep: what to cut, and what to measure if you really want mise en place. Then you cook, step by step. Each step highlights the ingredients it uses, and every cooking time is tappable to start a timer.

If your phone supports Apple Intelligence, cook mode shortens long steps so they can be read from across the kitchen, without losing any of the information you need.

Working with AI

Cook mode was designed on a canvas with Claude before any of it was built. I prompted with the following: turning the phone sideways into landscape would automatically enter cook mode, only the information needed at each step should be present, navigation should be trivial with a big next button and swipe available too. Claude showed a couple of options for handling the inline timers and I had it build them in a couple of ways so I could test each and get a feel for them. I aim for the least complicated solution and prefer using native tools, in this case the system timers, which makes them visible in the dynamic island or on your lock screen as well. Working with AI allows for the design process to be rapidly iterated, much like how a painter responds to how a paint lands on a canvas.

The name

When I was making the icon, Apple’s Image Playground drew a book with “Cookbo” written on it, and it stuck. I also like that you can’t spell cookbook without cookbo.

The website

I wanted the website to demonstrate its most handy feature, pulling the recipe from a source and storing it for you to keep in one place, just the recipe. Instead of just showing a looping animation of an example recipe, you can type in the URL of any recipe you like to see extracted and it will perform it for you.
